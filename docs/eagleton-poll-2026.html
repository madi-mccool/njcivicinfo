Rutgers-Eagleton Poll · October 2026

New Jerseyans Value Reliable Local Information. Many Can’t Find It.

A statewide poll of 1,004 adults, commissioned by the Consortium, on what residents need to know about their communities, how well local news delivers it and how information shapes civic life.

Key Findings

At a Glance

Results from a statewide poll of New Jersey adults conducted Sept. 24–27, 2026.

77% say reliable public safety and emergency information is “very important”
36% looked for local information in the past year and found it hard to find
42% say there aren’t enough local news sources in their area
46% vs. 18% read news coverage of a public meeting vs. attended or watched one
60% would be more likely to take part in local government if information were easier to find
What Matters

Reliable Information Residents Want

Half to three-quarters of New Jerseyans say reliable information is “very important” on nearly every local issue asked about.

How important is it to have reliable information about…

“New Jerseyans place a high value on reliable information about their local area, particularly on issues most pertinent to their everyday lives.”

— Ashley Koning, Director, Eagleton Center for Public Interest Polling
Finding Information

More Than a Third Say It Was Hard to Find

Difficulty finding local information crosses party lines: 35% of Democrats, 38% of independents and 35% of Republicans say what they looked for was hard to find.

In the past 12 months, have you looked for information about something happening in your local community?

What kind of information was hard to find?

Asked of the 36% who said information was hard to find (380 respondents). Respondents could choose more than one.

“Many New Jerseyans are actively seeking out local information, but more than a third say what they were looking for was hard to find. That is not a partisan divide.”

— Ashley Koning
Local News

Mixed Marks for Local News

A majority say local news keeps them at least somewhat well informed, but residents are divided on whether there are enough local news sources where they live.

How well does local news in your area keep you informed of the most important stories and issues of the day?

Are there too many, just the right amount or not enough local news sources in your area?

Democrats (49%) are more likely than independents (36%) to say there aren’t enough local news sources. Residents 65 and older (50%) and 50 to 64 (44%) are more likely than those 18 to 34 (32%) to say so.

Civic Life

Following Local Government Through the News

More than twice as many New Jerseyans read news coverage of a public meeting as attended or watched one.

In the past 12 months, have you done any of the following?

How much more likely would you be to take part in local government if information about public meetings and decisions were easier to find and understand?

“Residents want reporting that helps them understand decisions affecting their schools, neighborhoods and daily lives. Investing in local journalism means giving people the information they need to participate in and strengthen their democracy.”

— Lisa Sahulka, Executive Director, New Jersey Civic Information Consortium

About the Poll

Methodology

Results are from a statewide poll of 1,004 adults contacted through the probability-based Rutgers-Eagleton/SSRS Garden State Panel from Sept. 24 to 27, 2026. The survey was conducted online in English and Spanish. Data were weighted to represent the residential adult population of New Jersey. The full sample has a margin of error of +/- 4.1 percentage points, including the design effect; margins of error for subgroups are larger.

The poll was conducted by the Eagleton Center for Public Interest Polling (ECPIP) at Rutgers University–New Brunswick. Questions covered in this release were commissioned and paid for by the New Jersey Civic Information Consortium. Ashley Koning and Jessica Roman led analysis and preparation of the release.
